Mind - RSI plan

Plan for rebuilding how Claude Code is run here, written 21 October 2026 by the Mind session. Research and planning only. Nothing in the harness was changed except two codified orders Ivo gave during the run (section 13).

Mode labels on every claim: [MEASURED] a number this session computed from logs or transcripts; [READ] a quote from a fetched primary page, URL given; [REPORT] taken from one of our own earlier reports, named; [INFERENCE] our reading of measured facts, a design choice until its stage measures it; [SIMULATED] a hypothesis from the simulated expert pass, never evidence; [NOT DOCUMENTED] looked for and not found.

0. The answer

The harness does its job, but it grew by stacking. It has 213 tool folders, 134 gate scripts registered 154 times across four hook events plus 17 direct hooks, 162 always-on law bullets and 2,147 memory notes, and no step that ever removes anything [MEASURED]. The bill is measurable. In the last 7 days hooks held sessions for 68 hours, half of it one repo gate that timed out on 3,032 of its 3,033 runs and so never judged anything [MEASURED]. Every turn starts with about 137 thousand tokens of prefix before any work [MEASURED]. A third of everything the model emits is a read-only inspection call that a script could have made, and 6,031 gate refusals in 14 days each cost a whole turn [MEASURED]. 15 percent of all recorded lessons are about the gates themselves, false refusals or missing ones [MEASURED].

The Mind replaces stacking with a loop. One ledger holds every event. One scoreboard shows every cost per area each day. Laws live in layers so the prefix carries only what every turn needs. A gate must earn the right to deny in a shadow period and loses it when it is overridden. Standing self-checks look for their own upgrades: which tool is still the best, which law is still true, which gate still earns its keep, what is paid every turn for nothing, and what work needs no inference at all (de-agenting). The in-house bb tools stay, shrunk onto Claude Code's own primitives and one shared store; no outside framework covers their shape.

Targets, each against its measured baseline: half the prefix, half the hook wait, half the refusals, half the inspection turns, no capability lost, no new wait added. Six decisions need Ivo (section 13). Everything else can be started this week in the staged order of section 11.

The ten changes that matter most

  1. Kill the dead hooks first: the repo's session_worktree gate (3,032 of 3,033 runs killed at its 30 s limit, median 37.7 s per run, 2,063 min in 7 days), skill_catalog (916 of 916 killed), obsidian_start (985 of 2,471 killed) and worktree_setup on SubagentStart (47 of 48 killed) [MEASURED]. A hook killed at its timeout has its output discarded [READ, hooks.md], so the first judged one run in seven days and the others judged a third or less of theirs.
  2. De-agent the clock, the ceremonies and the shell-fix loop: 1,504 identical date calls, 8,515 bookkeeping turns and 2,711 shell-syntax refusals in 14 days become hook-injected facts, one close command and a PreToolUse rewrite [MEASURED, section 7].
  3. Split the prefix into layers: L0 under 12 thousand characters, area rules loaded by path, per-prompt injections sent only when they change [INFERENCE on the measured 136,561-token first request].
  4. One ledger (SQLite, write-ahead log, fsync) replaces the eight state stores that today answer "what is open and who waits on whom" [MEASURED inventory, section 6].
  5. A gate lifecycle: shadow for 7 days or 50 would-deny events, at least 95 percent labelled precision, p90 under 50 ms, demotion to shadow when the labelled precision falls under the floor, retirement after 30 days of zero true denies [SIMULATED H3, adopted as the design; numbers are starting values to be measured]. The override rate is a review signal, never the demotion key: overrides partly measure agent impatience, so keying on them demotes the most-bypassed gates first (round 1 with a colleague's agent, section 14).
  6. The codify loop becomes automatic: detect from transcripts, cluster into the 18 patterns, name the producing code, land on the lowest rung that holds: first mend the code itself, then reshape the interface, then a test, then advice, a deny only after those, and a written law solely where judgment is needed and no code can carry it.
  7. Setup tokens for the five rotating seats; the owner seat stays on /login for Remote Control and claude.ai connectors [READ, section 8].
  8. Spawns at their floor by construction: 429 visual-judge spawns in 14 days ran off their lane [MEASURED; corrected by the S1 stage on 21 Oct: 404 were explicit Opus overrides in the calling prompt, the 25 with no model took the lane's pin]; the effort and model become part of the lane file, not the prompt, and the spawn guard pins an unpinned generic spawn in code (updatedInput) instead of refusing it, one turn saved per spawn (round 1, section 14).
  9. A standard helper library instead of per-session scratch scripts: story.json written 52 times, judge rubrics about 90 times, memory-trim and pointer scripts rewritten in 20 sessions [MEASURED].
  10. The teacher becomes the proposal engine of the loop and applies generator fixes behind tests itself; today the teacher made 23 proposals and 5 were acted on [REPORT, Harness Writer 10-15].

9. The Mind: design [INFERENCE unless marked]

Principles taken from the sources: the simplest thing that works, complexity only when measured [READ, building-effective-agents]; every harness part encodes an assumption about what the model cannot do, so each part is tested for whether it is still needed [READ, harness-design]; evals of 20 to 50 tasks drawn from real failures, both when a behaviour should occur and when it should not [READ, evals essay]; an archive of past results read by later attempts, natural-language reflection, and an empirical check before a change is kept [READ abstracts of DGM, SICA, GEPA, Reflexion, ACE; the cross-paper synthesis is INFERENCE]; incremental deltas instead of whole-file rewrites to avoid brevity bias and context collapse [READ, ACE abstract].

9.1 Parts

  1. Ledger. One SQLite file, write-ahead log, fsync: tables for sessions, spawns, tool calls (summary), gate verdicts, tasks, decisions, promises, lessons, changes, measurements. Writers: one PostToolUse hook, one Stop hook, the launcher, the teacher. Today's eight stores (the task board, the decisions pool, the promises ledger, the attention list, the session tree, the automations history, the sweep entries, the asks ledger) become views on it. Native OpenTelemetry events [READ, monitoring-usage.md] feed the same file through a local collector. Write protocol (round 1, section 14): every event carries a ULID id and the writer's session id; writes are idempotent (an insert whose id exists is ignored, so a hook firing twice leaves one row); a versions table carries forward-only migrations; the eight stores stay as mirrors for two weeks while a reconciliation runs each night, comparing ids and counts in both directions, and the cut-over needs seven days of zero diff.
  2. Scoreboard. One page a day, one row per area: prefix tokens per first request as a spread by toolset and cache state (median and p90 per cell, never one number), hook wait p50 and p90 per event, hooks killed, refusals and override rate per gate, spawn refusals, cache-bust tokens, tokens per day, inspection share, ceremony share, corrections per 100 prompts, verified-done rows per attention hour. The ratchet generalised: a ceiling per row that only falls; a break is a ledger event the loop picks up.
  3. Codify loop, automatic. Detect (correction turns, deny-then-override, the same tool error twice, rework within 24 hours, ratchet breaks) → cluster (attach to one of the 18 roots before creating a new one) → root cause (name the producing code: tool, template, default, brief) → land on the lowest rung that works (first mend the code itself; then reshape the interface so the mistake has no way to be written; then a test; then advice; a deny only after those; a written law solely where judgment is needed and no code can carry it) → gate lifecycle (a shadow period of 7 days or 50 would-be denies, precision at least 95 percent on a labelled sample, both-way fixtures in CI, p90 under 50 ms, demotion to shadow when the labelled precision of the last 50 denies falls under 95 percent, an override rate past 5 percent does no more than queue those denies for labelling, retirement after 30 days of zero true denies, a law never cited in 60 days to the archive) → verification chain, explicit (tests; a reviewer of a second model family on the exact head; CI on the exact head sha, completed, never queued; merge; a READY verdict carries the exact head and the list of completed checks) → memory and ledger. A denied call is never re-fired verbatim: the next call differs or asks; a refusal-retry guard holds it (section 3.3 counted 1,193 such near-identical retries in 14 days). Both from round 1, section 14. Ivo approves only consent, money, send, credential and seal gates, promotions from shadow to deny, and a weekly one-screen harness diff. [SIMULATED H3 adopted as the design; its thresholds are starting values to be measured.]
  4. One dispatch. One resident process per event type with the gates imported once (no per-gate interpreter start), lanes in parallel as the product does [READ, hooks.md], a 50 ms p90 budget per gate, and the repo hooks moved under the same dispatcher so no hook can time out silently. Every hook's manifest entry declares what it writes (paths or stores) or marks it pure, and a test regenerates the lane table from those declarations, so parallel lanes never race on one file; a hook that overruns its box denies (fail closed) and the dispatcher never drops that denial (round 1, section 14). The first act is to remove the four hooks of section 0 item 1.
  5. Law store in layers. L0, the prefix: identity, the 13 stop-sign laws, the router, under 12 thousand characters. L1, area laws in files loaded by path or tool (laws/, .claude/rules), each with a "last cited" stamp. L2, gates as code with tests and lifecycle. L3, memory and the ledger. Per-prompt injections (the graph reminder is about 10 KB on every prompt, at the hook output cap of 10,000 characters [READ]) are sent once per session and again only when their content changes.
  6. De-agenting layer: section 7.2, D1 to D10, built as hooks and a helper library. Every injected fact (D1 clock, D2 pack, D9 context pack) carries its as-of time and its source, and the model goes back to the source whenever that stamp is absent or old; D6 includes a durability check before any scratch deletion (receipts, a real backup, a restore check); peer messages are typed envelopes (kind, from, to, refs, body) validated by a hook, relays included (round 1, section 14).
  7. Standing inference classes: section 7, last paragraph; each a nightly query with a threshold, an owner rung and a scoreboard row.
  8. Teacher and writer: the proposal engine of the loop; proposals carry ledger evidence; generator fixes and retirements ship behind tests without asking; everything else waits for the weekly diff.
